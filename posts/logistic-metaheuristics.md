# Metaheuristics for Logistics Optimization

Metaheuristics are high-level problem-solving frameworks that guide lower-level heuristics to escape local optima in complex optimization problems. For logistics—where VRP, scheduling, and allocation problems are NP-hard—metaheuristics are the practical workhorses.

**Simulated Annealing (SA)** mimics the metallurgical annealing process. Start with a high "temperature" that allows random, often worsening, moves. Gradually cool the temperature, reducing the probability of accepting worse solutions. The cooling schedule—exponential, logarithmic, or adaptive—determines search quality. SA's strength is simplicity: a VRP variant can be solved with just a neighbor operator (e.g., 2-opt for route improvement, relocate for moving customers) and a temperature schedule. SA reliably finds good solutions but converges slowly on large instances.

**Tabu Search** maintains a short-term memory of recently visited solutions or move attributes, preventing the search from cycling back. The tabu list (typically 10-50 entries) bans revisiting recent states, forcing exploration of new regions. Aspiration criteria override the tabu status if a forbidden move leads to the best solution found so far. For logistics, Tabu Search with adaptive memory (remembering good solution components across runs) often outperforms SA on tightly constrained problems like VRP with time windows.

**Genetic Algorithms (GA)** maintain a population of solutions evolving through selection, crossover, and mutation. Route representation uses permutation encoding (ordered list of customers). Crossover operators like PMX (partially mapped crossover) preserve route structure while combining parent solutions. Mutation swaps or reverses route segments. GA's population diversity maintains exploration, but the crossover operator for logistics problems is difficult to design—a poor crossover produces invalid routes (visiting a customer twice or not at all) that require repair.

**Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)** simulates pheromone trails. Artificial ants construct solutions probabilistically, depositing pheromone on good route segments. Future ants follow high-pheromone paths. Pheromone evaporation prevents premature convergence. ACO is particularly effective for dynamic logistics problems where new orders arrive during execution—ants can adapt to changing conditions by updating pheromone levels. The downside: slow convergence compared to population-based methods.

**Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS)** systematically changes neighborhood structures during search. Start with a simple neighborhood (swap two customers), apply local search to find a local optimum, then switch to a more complex neighborhood (3-opt, cross-exchange) to escape. VNS requires multiple neighborhood definitions but has few parameters to tune, making it practical for real-world logistics where problem structure varies daily.

Hybrid metaheuristics—combining population methods with local search (memetic algorithms)—consistently outperform pure approaches. The practical recommendation: start with Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) for logistics, which learns which destroy/repair operators work best for your specific problem instance during the search.
